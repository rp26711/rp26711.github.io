My PGCert ePortfolio Project

Project

Evaluation of the final project (Unit 11) vs. the initial project proposal (Unit 6)


This project enabled me to critically reflect on database development, data analytics and AI in a realistic context. I developed a stronger understanding of how data quality, database design and cleaning influence analytical reliability, supported by Huxley’s (2020) discussion of data preparation. Working with real-world data highlighted the importance of credible, evidence-based findings.

Both tasks explored AI in database management and analytics but differed in emphasis. Unit 6 focused on database design and selecting appropriate AI tools, while Unit 11 demonstrated their practical application through a real dataset and dashboard.

In Unit 6, I proposed an AI-supported data management pipeline and dashboard to help an events company understand Did Not Finish (DNF) results. I achieved 55%, with feedback identifying the need for deeper critical analysis, stronger business alignment, and database design models and prototypes. Reflecting on this feedback, I recognised that their absence weakened the connection between my proposed solution and the client’s requirements. My approach was too descriptive and insufficiently demonstrated how the solution would work in practice.

Consequently, I revisited my proposal before developing the Unit 11 dashboard, strengthening the implementation approach. I focused on explaining data-cleaning decisions, communicating findings to non-technical audiences and addressing GDPR considerations. However, I recognised that the dashboard identified descriptive relationships rather than causal explanations for runners’ DNFs. Further investigation would therefore be necessary before making targeted recommendations, reflecting the limitations of descriptive analytics in establishing causation (Irizarry, 2020).

Using AI, particularly ChatGPT, was another significant learning experience. Although AI can improve efficiency and support coding and technical problem-solving (Hao et al., 2024), I recognised the importance of human oversight in validating outputs and ensuring findings were evidence-based. This reinforced the value of a Human–AI–Human approach, where human judgement guides analysis and critically evaluates AI-generated outputs (Giray, 2026).

Overall, Unit 6 established the proposed framework, while Unit 11 developed my understanding of applying AI-supported analytics to business decisions. The project strengthened my technical confidence, independence and critical thinking, while challenging team dynamics improved my adaptability and communication. Most importantly, I learned that successful data projects require more than technical implementation; they demand careful planning, critical evaluation and justified decision-making.
