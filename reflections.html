Professional Reflections

Module Reflections

This reflection was guided by Rolfe, Freshwater and Jasper’s (2001) framework, structured around ‘What?’, ‘So what?’ and ‘Now what?’ to describe learning, analyse its significance and consider its application to future practice. Weekly summaries, a professional matrix and a development plan supported this process and are featured in this ePortfolio.

What?

Throughout this module, I developed my understanding of Big Data technologies, including the five Vs and the challenges associated with data quality, integrity, security and scalability (Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda, 2024). I also developed practical skills in using Python and pandas to clean, transform, merge and analyse datasets (McKinney, 2022). In addition, I explored web scraping and APIs as methods of data acquisition and integration. Through formative tasks and independent reading, I strengthened my technical capabilities and theoretical perspectives.

My understanding of relational database design improved, particularly regarding how normalisation, including Third Normal Form (3NF), reduces data redundancy and how primary and foreign keys help maintain data integrity (Codd, 1972). I also explored ACID transaction properties, database recovery, backup strategies, and batch and stream processing. Furthermore, examining Lambda and Kappa architectures, alongside lakehouse models, enhanced my understanding of modern data-processing approaches (Microsoft, 2025). Exploring the future of data pipeline automation and emerging developments in DataOps also broadened my understanding of how data engineering practices are evolving towards greater efficiency, scalability, and operational reliability (Woodie, 2019). Further learning explored GDPR, data protection, privacy, and organisational accountability, highlighting the importance of ethical and regulatory considerations in the development and management of data-driven systems (Burgess, 2024; Bhatnagar and Gajjar, 2024). This extended my understanding of security and emerging data science trends.

The most rewarding, yet challenging, tasks were the data-cleaning exercises and team project involving database design and an AI-assisted dashboard. These required me to develop Python cleansing code, design 3NF diagrams and create dashboard prototypes, while using AI to troubleshoot errors and refine my work in response to feedback. Both tasks strengthened my ability to apply theoretical knowledge to practical challenges.

So What?

Initially, I focused on understanding techniques and completing tasks. However, data-cleaning exercises and McKinney’s (2022) work highlighted the importance of data quality in producing reliable analysis. Developing my AI-assisted dashboard challenged my assumption that publicly available, peer-reviewed data required minimal cleaning. Using Python and pandas to address missing values and inconsistent data types reinforced that cleaning decisions must reflect the dataset’s purpose and intended analysis, rather than relying on standard techniques alone. This demonstrated that technical proficiency is insufficient without critical judgement about data suitability and quality (Huxley, 2020).

Similarly, developing 3NF diagrams strengthened my understanding of how normalisation reduces redundancy and supports data integrity (Codd, 1972). Initially, I struggled to translate database theory into a practical design. The normalisation tasks and database development in Unit 7 helped me visualise relationships and clarify project requirements, demonstrating that I could apply concepts rather than simply describe them. However, effective designs must also accommodate business requirements and performance.

The team project was particularly challenging when my team member left close to the deadline. Losing practical support and a sounding board for ideas left me feeling overwhelmed, because I lacked a technical background. Tutor feedback that my report was too descriptive and lacked a design model initially undermined my confidence. However, developing diagrams and dashboard prototypes in response to this feedback before creating the final dashboard helped me address these weaknesses and connect my written explanations with practical evidence. This demonstrated the value of iterative development, although responding to feedback earlier could have reduced the pressure near the deadline. I recognised that my emotional response to criticism could limit my willingness to evaluate my work objectively. Learning to treat feedback as an opportunity for improvement strengthened my understanding and motivation.

Maintaining meeting notes, documenting ideas and organising deadlines helped me transition from working within a team environment to working independently. Nevertheless, working alone limited opportunities to challenge assumptions and identify problems collaboratively. Although independence strengthened my self-management, it could not replace the benefits of shared problem-solving. In future group projects, I will clarify responsibilities, communicate difficulties earlier and establish contingency plans to reduce the impact of unexpected changes.

My use of AI to support Python programming developed my technical confidence. The human–AI–human approach (Giray, 2026) encouraged me to assess solutions rather than accept them automatically, reinforcing my responsibility for the accuracy of my work. However, AI assistance is only beneficial when I understand and verify its outputs; otherwise, it risks creating dependence and introducing errors I may not recognise. My learning about GDPR, API security and responsible data governance further demonstrated that effective analytics must balance innovation with privacy, confidentiality and accountability (Burgess, 2024). Rather than treating these considerations as secondary requirements, I recognised that safeguards must be integrated throughout development. Using AI to develop my dashboard helped me understand these responsibilities in practice, highlighting that successful data-driven development requires not only technical capability but also critical judgement and ethical accountability.

Now What?

Looking ahead, revisiting my skills matrix and development plan is essential to evaluating my progress against my initial objectives My commitment to strengthening my technical skills and applying them to conceptual ideas is evidenced by the artefacts I have developed, particularly my AI-assisted dashboard and Python data-cleaning scripts. Building on this progress, I will ensure future practice prioritises data quality, decision documentation, regulatory compliance and security. As technologies continue to evolve, I must harness their innovative potential without compromising privacy, confidentiality or responsible data management. Across the technologies explored, maintaining data integrity and upholding security standards have emerged as fundamental principles underpinning effective implementation.

Reflecting on my skills matrix, I initially rated my teamwork capabilities highly. However, the circumstances of my team project limited my opportunity to develop and demonstrate these skills fully. Nevertheless, this experience reinforced the importance of collaboration and not taking opportunities for teamwork for granted. Although working independently strengthened my adaptability, it also exposed the limitations of working without diverse perspectives and constructive challenge. Moving forward, I will develop contingency plans to manage unexpected challenges and approach criticism as an opportunity for improvement rather than a judgement of my abilities. I will also critically evaluate my decisions and emotional responses to identify areas for continued development and strengthen my professional judgement.

Overall, professional competence requires reflection, adaptability and critical judgement beyond technical knowledge. Applying Rolfe, Freshwater and Jasper’s (2001) framework has enabled me to recognise how experiences can inform future practice. I will use these insights to make informed decisions, respond constructively to challenges and maintain a responsible, security-conscious approach to data management as technologies and industry practices continue to evolve.
