My PGCert ePortfolio Unit Summaries

Unit Summaries

Unit 1 – Introduction to Big Data Technologies and Data Management This week developed my understanding of Big Data through the five Vs: volume, velocity, variety, value and veracity (Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda, 2024). I recognised that its value depends not only on scale, but also on data quality, reliability and security. Huxley (2020) demonstrated how inadequate data cleaning, including unresolved missing, duplicate or inaccurate data, can undermine analytical validity. McKinney (2022) introduced Python as a practical tool for data manipulation, cleaning and analysis. Applying these concepts to IoT highlighted the complexity of continuously generated, diverse datasets. Overall, I developed a more critical understanding of how effective data management enables reliable insights while addressing quality and security challenges.

Unit 2 – Introduction to Data Types and Formats This week developed my understanding of how data types and formats influence the storage, processing and analysis of Big Data. Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda (2024) demonstrated how technologies such as Hadoop, MapReduce, Hive and NoSQL address the limitations of traditional data-processing approaches. McKinney (2022) further developed my practical understanding of Python data structures, file handling and data parsing, including the use of pandas to access data from files, databases and APIs. Critically, I recognised that structured, semi-structured, quasi-structured and unstructured data present different challenges for interoperability, processing and management. Applying these concepts through Python and peer discussion strengthened my ability to evaluate appropriate tools and techniques for data wrangling, particularly within complex IoT environments.

Unit 3 – Data Collection and Storage This week developed my understanding of different data sources and the importance of data quality, integrity and reliability. McKinney (2022) demonstrated how Python and pandas can support data cleaning through handling missing values, removing duplicates and transforming data. Darius (2024) highlighted the challenges of managing large IoT datasets and the importance of scalable cloud solutions, filtering and appropriate data storage. I also gained practical experience of web scraping using Python, Requests and BeautifulSoup to collect and structure online data. Overall, I developed a stronger understanding of how effective data management, fact-checking and data cleansing can support reliable analysis and decision-making.

Unit 4 – Data Cleaning and Transformation This week developed my understanding of data cleaning, transformation and the data management pipeline. McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python and pandas, including handling missing and duplicate data, string manipulation and data transformation. Microsoft (2025) developed my understanding of Big Data architectures, including Lambda, Kappa and Lakehouse approaches for managing data ingestion, storage, processing and analysis. I applied these concepts through the data cleaning lecturecast, Data Management Pipeline Test and UNICEF labour dataset exercise, improving my ability to clean and transform data using the pipeline as a guide. I also developed a greater understanding of good Python practices and the requirements for effective data design and process automation. Overall, I recognised that although Big Data involves larger and more complex architectures, the fundamental principles of data cleaning and management remain similar.

Unit 5 – Data Cleaning and Automating Data Collections This week developed my understanding of data cleaning, automation and the data management pipeline. I applied Python to household survey data, including creating and converting files into CSV format, web scraping and developing scripts to automate cleaning processes. Mahato et al (2024) strengthened my understanding of conversational AI and machine learning in Big Data systems, including personalised customer experiences and crop yield prediction. Woodie (2019) developed my understanding of DataOps and the automation of data engineering tasks through end-to-end pipelines. A seminar further developed my learning outcomes by exploring challenges, security issues, risks and limitations in data wrangling, as well as fact-finding, cloud computing, and appropriate tools and methodologies for preparing, cleaning, exploring and evaluating datasets. Overall, I developed a clearer understanding of how data processes can be automated and scaled using machine learning, programming and analytical tools.

Unit 6 – Database Design and Normalisation This week developed my understanding of relational database design and the importance of structuring data effectively. The lecturecast strengthened my understanding of primary and foreign keys, database anomalies and normalisation through 1NF, 2NF and 3NF, helping me recognise how database structure affects data integrity. McKinney (2022) further developed my understanding of using Python with real-world datasets, including data cleansing, JSON files, list comprehensions, pandas, merging datasets, filling values and the explode method. I also explored matplotlib and seaborn for data visualisation. Completing the project report encouraged me to consider how these techniques apply to real-world databases, particularly business requirements, data quality and system limitations. Overall, I developed a stronger appreciation of how effective database design and data preparation support meaningful analysis and business decision-making.

Unit 7 – Constructing Normalised Tables and Database Build This unit developed my understanding of relational database design and the importance of structuring data effectively. I strengthened my ability to identify data attributes, relationships, functional dependencies and constraints, while learning how to transform a flat-file database into a normalised relational model. Through the practical tasks, I normalised data to Third Normal Form (3NF) and built a relational database system with linked tables, demonstrating my understanding of primary and foreign keys. I also tested the database to ensure referential integrity, identify potential errors and anomalies, and evaluate the reliability and usability of the data. Chen (2023) further developed my understanding of database design, normalisation and implementation, highlighting the importance of creating well-structured and efficient systems. Taipalus (2024) encouraged me to reflect on database performance testing, particularly the importance of realistic testing scenarios and clearly reported results. Overall, I developed a stronger appreciation of how effective database design, data integrity and systematic testing contribute to reliable data management, while recognising the importance of applying these principles to real-world requirements.

Unit 8 – Compliance and Regulatory Framework for Managing Data This unit developed my understanding of data protection regulations and the importance of compliance for organisations and data stakeholders. The lecturecast strengthened my knowledge of compliance requirements, regulatory frameworks and the responsibilities associated with data storage and security. Burgess (2024) further developed my understanding of GDPR principles, individual rights regarding personal data and automated decision-making. I also explored applicable standards, security requirements and exemptions across different organisations and industries. Completing the collaborative discussion post encouraged me to compare GDPR security requirements with other relevant compliance laws and consider how organisations protect personal data while meeting their legal obligations. Overall, I developed a stronger appreciation of how effective data protection practices, regulatory compliance and individual rights support responsible data management and organisational accountability.

Unit 9 – Database Management Systems (DBMS) and Models This unit developed my understanding of database design principles and the importance of selecting appropriate database management systems (DBMS) for different application environments. The lecturecast strengthened my understanding of database architecture, implementation and security, while Tejada (2024) highlighted the importance of choosing appropriate data processing architectures, including batch processing, streaming, Lambda, Kappa and lakehouse approaches. The seminar developed my ability to critically evaluate data wrangling challenges and consider suitable tools and techniques for preparing, cleaning, processing and analysing large datasets. Providing feedback to peers on GDPR and personal data security further encouraged me to reflect on data protection requirements and international compliance practices. Overall, I developed a stronger appreciation of how effective database design, appropriate programming paradigms and robust security measures support reliable data processing and informed decision-making.

Unit 10 – More on APIs (Application Programming Interfaces) for Data Parsing

Learning Objectives

This unit focused on the role of Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) in enabling systems and applications to communicate, exchange data, and support data parsing across different platforms. It examined how APIs can be configured to provide connectivity while considering the security measures required to maintain reliable and resilient functionality. The unit also evaluated the practical challenges and potential issues that can arise when implementing APIs, including the need to balance accessibility, security, and effective data communication.

Unit 11 – DBMS Transaction and Recovery

Learning Objectives

This unit explored transaction processing and the mechanisms used to maintain database reliability, consistency, and data integrity when system failures occur. It examined the ACID properties of transactions and their role in ensuring that database operations are completed correctly, while also considering scheduled transactions, system failures, checkpoints, and the transaction cycle. The unit further developed an understanding of transaction managers and how they coordinate fast-moving and interleaved transactions to maintain a consistent database state.

Unit 12 – Future of Big Data Analytics

Learning Objectives

This unit examined the evolving role of machine learning in advancing Big Data analytics, with a focus on emerging technologies and future trends in the analysis of large and complex datasets. It explored the application of machine learning strategies in data modelling and considered how these developments may be applied within organisational contexts. The unit also revisited the importance of compliance frameworks, including regulations, laws, rules, and standards, in ensuring that data relating to individuals is managed responsibly and protected from breaches.
