My PGCert ePortfolio Unit Summaries

Unit Summaries

Unit 1 – Introduction to Big Data Technologies and Data Management This unit, I explored Big Data’s five Vs (Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda, 2024), recognising how data quality and security influence analytical validity (Huxley, 2020). McKinney (2022) demonstrated Python’s role in data manipulation and cleaning. Overall, the unit deepened my understanding of how Big Data, Python and IoT concepts work together to support effective data management and reliable insights from complex datasets.

Unit 2 – Introduction to Data Types and Formats This unit, I explored how data types and formats influence Big Data processing and analysis (Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda, 2024). McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python data structures, file handling and pandas. Overall, the unit developed my understanding of how data processing and wrangling techniques support effective analysis of complex IoT datasets.

Unit 3 – Data Collection and Storage This unit, I explored data sources and the importance of data quality, integrity and reliability. McKinney (2022) demonstrated how Python and pandas support data cleaning, while Darius (2024) highlighted scalable IoT data management. Overall, the unit developed my understanding of how data collection, cleaning and validation contribute to reliable analysis and informed decision-making.



Unit 4 – Data Cleaning and Transformation This unit, I explored data cleaning, transformation and management pipelines. McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python and pandas, while Microsoft (2025) introduced Lambda, Kappa and Lakehouse architectures. Overall, the unit developed my understanding of how data-management techniques and architectures support efficient, reliable and scalable Big Data processing.



Unit 5 – Data Cleaning and Automating Data Collections This unit, I developed my understanding of data cleaning, automation and management pipelines through Python-based data processing. Mahato et al. (2024) explored machine learning applications in Big Data, while Woodie (2019) highlighted DataOps and automated pipelines. Overall, the unit strengthened my understanding of how data-wrangling techniques, security and cloud computing contribute to reliable and scalable data processing.


Unit 6 – Database Design and Normalisation This unit, I explored relational database design, normalisation and data integrity. McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python, pandas, data cleansing, dataset merging and visualisation. Overall, the unit developed my understanding of how effective database design and data preparation support reliable analysis and informed business decisions.



Unit 7 – Constructing Normalised Tables and Database Build This unit strengthened my understanding of relational database design, normalisation and referential integrity. Practical tasks developed my ability to transform flat files into 3NF relational models using linked tables and keys. Chen (2023) reinforced effective database implementation, while Taipalus (2024) highlighted systematic performance testing. Overall, the unit developed my understanding of how database design, implementation and testing contribute to reliable and efficient data management.


Unit 8 – Compliance and Regulatory Framework for Managing Data This unit strengthened my understanding of data protection regulations, GDPR principles and organisational accountability. Burgess (2020) developed my awareness of individual data rights, automated decision-making and compliance requirements. Overall, the unit deepened my understanding of how legal compliance and effective security practices support responsible data management and protect personal information.



Unit 9 – Database Management Systems (DBMS) and Models This unit strengthened my understanding of database design, DBMS selection and data-processing architectures. Revisiting Microsoft (2025) reinforced my understanding of batch, streaming, Lambda, Kappa and lakehouse approaches. Seminar discussions and peer feedback on GDPR developed my awareness of data-wrangling techniques, data protection and compliance. Overall, the unit deepened my understanding of how effective database design, data processing and security support reliable analysis and informed decision-making.



Unit 10 – More on APIs (Application Programming Interfaces) for Data Parsing This unit strengthened my understanding of APIs, data parsing and system integration. Schiller and Larochelle (2024) highlighted the importance of business requirements, security, privacy and adaptability in data engineering. Exploring IBM QRadar and Google Maps APIs developed my awareness of data connectivity, integration and security risks. Overall, the unit deepened my understanding of how secure and reliable APIs support effective data exchange and system integration.


Unit 11 – DBMS Transaction and Recovery This unit strengthened my understanding of emerging Big Data analytics and machine learning applications. Bhatnagar and Gajjar (2024) highlighted AI’s ethical, regulatory and privacy challenges, while Himeur et al. (2022) demonstrated its applications in intelligent building management. Overall, the unit developed my understanding of AI’s opportunities, limitations and organisational implications, highlighting the importance of continually updating my knowledge as these technologies evolve.


Unit 12 – Future of Big Data Analytics This unit strengthened my understanding of emerging Big Data analytics and machine learning applications. Bhatnagar and Gajjar (2024) highlighted AI’s ethical, regulatory and privacy challenges, while Himeur et al. (2022) demonstrated its applications in intelligent building management. Reflecting on these developments reinforced the importance of continually updating my knowledge to evaluate AI’s opportunities, limitations and organisational implications.
