Unit Summaries

Unit 1 – Introduction to Big Data Technologies and Data Management

Learning Objectives

This unit provided an introduction to the key features, technologies, and concepts underpinning Big Data. It explored the exponential growth of data through an evaluation of the 4/5 Vs of Big Data, considering both the advantages and risks associated with increasingly complex datasets and technologies. The unit also introduced the critical analysis of Big Data methodologies and technologies, alongside an examination of data security requirements and Big Data management systems.

Reading Summaries

Huxley (2020) explored the importance of data cleaning and quality checking of datasets to ensure reliable analysis. The study discussed approaches to handling duplicates, missing values, extreme values, and inaccurate data. It also highlighted the importance of researchers documenting their data-cleaning processes to provide transparency and an accurate representation of the data used in the analysis. This reading reinforced the importance of data quality as a fundamental stage of the data science process, particularly because poor-quality data can directly affect the reliability and validity of subsequent analysis. However, the perspective focused primarily on researchers' handling of data, rather than considering data cleaning and quality management within a real-world Big Data context. Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda (2024, Chapter 8, Sections 8.1–8.28) discussed the concept of Big Data through consideration of the five Vs: volume, velocity, variety, value and veracity. The reading identified key dimensions of Big Data, including real-time data, shared data, linked data and high-fidelity data. It also explored technologies associated with Big Data, including machine learning, cloud computing, fog computing and edge computing, alongside real-world applications such as smart homes, healthcare and smart cities. This reading provided a strong introduction to Big Data, its associated technologies and applications, and demonstrated how these technologies are increasingly being integrated into everyday life. However, while the reading identified ways in which Big Data can support cybersecurity, further consideration of the potential negative impacts and security risks associated with Big Data would provide a more balanced perspective.
