My PGCert ePortfolio Unit Summaries

Unit Summaries

Unit 1 – Introduction to Big Data Technologies and Data Management This week, I explored Big Data’s five Vs (Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda, 2024), recognising how data quality and security influence analytical validity (Huxley, 2020). McKinney (2022) demonstrated Python’s role in data manipulation and cleaning. Applying these concepts to IoT deepened my understanding of how effective data management supports reliable insights from complex datasets.

Unit 2 – Introduction to Data Types and Formats This week, I explored how data types and formats influence Big Data processing and analysis (Balaji, Sonowal and Gowda, 2024). McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python data structures, file handling and pandas. Applying these concepts developed my ability to evaluate data-wrangling techniques for complex IoT environments.

Unit 3 – Data Collection and Storage This week, I explored data sources and the importance of data quality, integrity and reliability. McKinney (2022) demonstrated how Python and pandas support data cleaning, while Darius (2024) highlighted scalable IoT data management. Practising web scraping with Requests and BeautifulSoup strengthened my understanding of collecting, validating and preparing reliable data for analysis and decision-making.

Unit 4 – Data Cleaning and Transformation This week, I explored data cleaning, transformation and management pipelines. McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python and pandas, while Microsoft (2025) introduced Lambda, Kappa and Lakehouse architectures. Applying these concepts to the UNICEF labour dataset improved my data-cleaning skills and understanding of automation, reinforcing how fundamental data-management principles underpin effective Big Data processing.

Unit 5 – Data Cleaning and Automating Data Collections This week, I developed my understanding of data cleaning, automation and management pipelines through Python-based household survey processing. Mahato et al. (2024) explored machine learning applications in Big Data, while Woodie (2019) highlighted DataOps and automated pipelines. Seminar discussions strengthened my understanding of data-wrangling risks, security, cloud computing and appropriate analytical tools for preparing reliable, scalable datasets.

Unit 6 – Database Design and Normalisation This week, I explored relational database design, normalisation and data integrity. McKinney (2022) strengthened my practical understanding of Python, pandas, data cleansing, dataset merging and visualisation. Applying these concepts through the project report developed my ability to evaluate how effective database design and data preparation support reliable analysis and informed business decisions.

Unit 7 – Constructing Normalised Tables and Database Build This unit strengthened my understanding of relational database design, normalisation and referential integrity. Practical tasks developed my ability to transform flat files into 3NF relational models using linked tables and keys. Chen (2023) reinforced effective database implementation, while Taipalus (2024) highlighted systematic performance testing. Overall, I recognised how structured design and testing support reliable data management.

Unit 8 – Compliance and Regulatory Framework for Managing Data This unit strengthened my understanding of data protection regulations, GDPR principles and organisational accountability. Burgess (2024) developed my awareness of individual data rights, automated decision-making and compliance requirements. Comparing GDPR with other regulations highlighted how effective security practices and legal compliance support responsible data management and protect personal information.

Unit 9 – Database Management Systems (DBMS) and Models This unit strengthened my understanding of database design, DBMS selection and data-processing architectures. Revisiting Microsoft (2024) reinforced my understanding of batch, streaming, Lambda, Kappa and lakehouse approaches. Seminar discussions developed my ability to evaluate data-wrangling techniques, while peer feedback on GDPR strengthened my awareness of data protection and compliance. Overall, I recognised how effective database design, processing and security support reliable analysis and informed decisions.

Unit 10 – More on APIs (Application Programming Interfaces) for Data Parsing This unit strengthened my understanding of APIs, data parsing and system integration. Schiller and Larochelle (2024) reinforced the importance of considering business requirements, security, privacy and adaptability in data engineering. Exploring IBM QRadar and Google Maps APIs developed my awareness of data connectivity, integration and security risks. Reflecting on peer discussions further reinforced the importance of secure, reliable APIs for effective data exchange.

Unit 11 – DBMS Transaction and Recovery This unit strengthened my understanding of transaction processing, ACID properties and database recovery. Learning about commit, rollback, logs and checkpoints highlighted how consistency and data integrity are maintained. McKinney (2022) reinforced effective data preparation using Python and pandas, while evaluating the Grandfather-Father-Son backup procedure developed my awareness of disaster recovery and security risks. Exploring Tableau further strengthened my understanding of data modelling and metadata management, highlighting how reliable data processes support informed decision-making.

Unit 12 – Future of Big Data Analytics This unit strengthened my understanding of emerging Big Data analytics and machine learning applications. Bhatnagar and Gajjar (2024) highlighted AI’s ethical, regulatory and privacy challenges, while Himeur et al. (2022) demonstrated its applications in intelligent building management. Reflecting on these developments reinforced the importance of continually updating my knowledge to evaluate AI’s opportunities, limitations and organisational implications.
